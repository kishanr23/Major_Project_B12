A small number of solar-powered LoRa mesh nodes are mounted at trail markers along the route; each node also functions as a Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) peripheral, allowing a hiker's smartphone — running a lightweight companion app installed before the hike — to connect directly to nearby nodes without requiring Wi-Fi, cellular signal, or any additional hardware. Once connected, the hiker can view an offline trail map, log a routine check-in, or send a digitally signed SOS that is relayed node-to-node toward a ranger or trailhead station, even when no direct radio path exists at the moment the alert is sent. The project is scoped as a focused, low-cost pilot: a handful of nodes placed along one trail, validated with real hikers and real field conditions before any larger investment is device (Raspberry Pi or equivalent) with a LoRa interface.Hiker's own smartphone with the companion app installed (no additional hardware required on the hiker's side).Abstract:Hiking trails frequently pass through terrain with no cellular signal, leaving an injured or lost hiker with no way to raise an alert until they reach coverage or are reported missing, often hours later. Existing alternatives — satellite messengers, dedicated mesh-radio devices, and manual trailhead sign-in sheets — either shift cost and effort onto the individual hiker or only surface problems after the fact. TrailGuard addresses this gap by placing the intelligence in the trail itself: a small number of solar-powered nodes, each combining a long-range LoRa radio with a Bluetooth Low Energy interface, are mounted at trail markers to form a mesh network. A hiker installs a lightweight companion app before setting out; the app connects to nearby nodes over BLE and provides an offline trail map, a check-in function, and a digitally signed SOS, together with live bearing-and-distance guidance back to the trail computed from each node's known coordinate and the phone's own GPS fix. Store-and-forward routing ensures alerts are delivered even through temporary coverage gaps, while adaptive sleep scheduling lets nodes run for long stretches on solar power without compromising emergency response time. Built on an adapted open-source Meshtastic platform, the initial deployment targets 3–5 nodes on a single trail to validate multi-hop delivery, SOS latency, bearing accuracy, and battery performance under real field conditions before wider rollout across a forest or protected area.Literature Survey (Minimum 5 recent research journals indexed in Scopus/Web of Science):Sl.
